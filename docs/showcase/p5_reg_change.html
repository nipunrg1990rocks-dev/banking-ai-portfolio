RegRadar: Regulatory Change Impact Analyzer

When a regulator publishes a new or amended rule, a crew of seven AI agents reads it, compares it word by word with the version it replaces, finds the bank policies and controls it lands on, decides where the bank no longer complies, scores the risk and drafts the fixes. Then it challenges its own work. A named person decides every action and a second person signs off.

The agent crew

Seven agents hand the work along, and a person decides

  1. Intake

    Splits the circular into provisions and pulls out every deadline, frequency and threshold.

  2. Change Analyst

    New, amended, removed or unchanged, with a redline and the figures that moved.

  3. Policy Mapper

    Hybrid search over 64 policy sections; searches again when the first match is weak.

  4. Gap Assessor

    Conflict, partial, missing or covered, with both sentences as evidence and a risk score.

  5. Remediation Planner

    Exact policy redlines, new clauses and control changes, with owners and due dates.

  6. Challenger

    Sends borderline calls to a person. A Databricks model gives a second opinion and can only add flags.

  7. Reporter

    Writes the executive summary; any number the model writes must exist in the findings.

  8. Human approver

    Accepts, edits or rejects each action. A different person signs off. Everything is in a hash-chained audit trail.

The banking problem

Regulatory change is a reading problem with a deadline

Banks receive hundreds of regulatory updates a year. For each one, someone has to find what changed, which policies and controls it touches, whether the bank still complies and what must be fixed before it takes effect. The expensive failures are quiet: a tightened figure nobody spotted, a policy updated while the control that enforces it still runs on the old number, or no record of who decided what. RegRadar does the first pass in seconds and keeps the decisions with accountable people.

Its first run scored 80% with one false alarm; both causes were fixed in a general way, and the first-run score is reported alongside. Policy citation accuracy is 82%.

AI controls built in

The model challenges; it doesn't decide

  • Verdicts come from explainable rules that give the same answer every time
  • The model's second opinion can add a review flag and can never change a verdict
  • Any number in a model summary or chat answer must exist in the sources, or it is rejected
  • Sign-off is blocked until every Critical and High action and every flag is decided
  • Four-eyes: the approver cannot be anyone who decided an action
  • Prompt-injection and personal-data guardrails on the chat
  • Runs fully offline when no model is connected, and says so
