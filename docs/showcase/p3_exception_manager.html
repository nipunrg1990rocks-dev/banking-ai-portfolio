Ledgerline Exception Manager

AI agents that work a bank's operations exception queue: they triage each case, investigate it with tools, score its priority and propose one fix. Plain, auditable rules then decide who must approve. Regulated decisions always go to a person.

Independent project built by Nipun Gupta with AI-assisted development tools. All customers, payments, staff and the sanctions list are synthetic and fictional. It is not affiliated with any employer.

The banking problem

The legwork is slow; the decision is the risk

Operations teams clear rejected payments, reconciliation breaks, sanctions hits, failed trades and suspense items every day. Most of the time goes on pulling evidence from five systems. The real risk is the decision: releasing a screened payment or changing beneficiary details without the right sign-off is a regulatory and fraud event. Automation helps only if it can prove who approved what.

How one exception moves

The agent proposes, controls decide, people approve

Intake & guardrails→ Triage→ Investigate with tools→ Priority score→ Propose one action→ Control gate→ Human approval (pauses)→ Execute & verify→ Hash-chained audit

A 12-step LangGraph workflow. When a person must decide, the graph saves its state and stops; it resumes when they approve, reject or escalate. Only low-value, reversible, non-regulated items may skip the human step.

Results from automated testing

100%Correct route on a 42-case test queue across 8 exception types
0Regulated decisions executed without a human
0 / 300Control violations in fuzzed cases, incl. prompt-injection text
280Approval attempts by the wrong person, all blocked

Each test case carries hand-written acceptance criteria the agent cannot see. With only free text (no reason codes) the triage model scores 81%, and every miss goes to a human rather than a wrong category. These checks confirm the design works as intended; they are not an independent benchmark.

Banking controls built in

Controls a reviewer would expect

  • Sanctions, KYC and credit decisions are never automated
  • Straight-through only at or below USD 10,000, reversible and non-regulated
  • Maker-checker above USD 50,000, with a delegated-authority matrix
  • Segregation of duties: different people for every signature, never the submitter
  • Agents can only read; one executor node acts, after a final authorisation check
  • PII redacted before any model or log; every step in a tamper-evident log
