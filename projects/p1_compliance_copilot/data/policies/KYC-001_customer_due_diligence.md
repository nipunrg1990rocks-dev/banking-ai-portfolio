# Know Your Customer and Customer Due Diligence Policy

This is a synthetic policy written for the fictional Ledgerline Bank. It is used for demonstration only and does not describe any real institution.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy sets the minimum standards Ledgerline Bank applies to identify and verify customers, understand the nature of customer relationships, and assess money laundering and terrorist financing risk. It applies to all retail, business and corporate banking customers, in all channels including branch, digital and relationship-managed onboarding.

## 2. Customer Identification Program

Before an account is opened, the bank must collect and verify the customer's full legal name, date of birth (for individuals), residential or registered address, and a government-issued identification number. Identity must be verified using documentary evidence (such as a passport or national identity card) or approved non-documentary electronic verification. An account must not be opened, and no transaction may be processed, until identity verification is complete. Onboarding staff must record the verification method and the reference of the evidence used.

## 3. Customer Risk Rating

Every customer must receive a risk rating of Low, Medium or High at onboarding. The rating is calculated by the customer risk rating model using customer type, geography, products and services, delivery channel, and expected transaction activity. Customers connected to a high-risk jurisdiction, cash-intensive businesses, money services businesses, and politically exposed persons are rated High by default unless an exception is approved by Financial Crime Compliance. Relationship managers may request a manual override of a model-generated rating, but every override must be documented with a rationale and approved by a second-line compliance officer.

## 4. Beneficial Ownership

For legal entity customers, the bank must identify and verify every individual who directly or indirectly owns 25 percent or more of the equity interests of the entity, and at least one individual with significant responsibility to control or manage the entity (the controlling person). For High risk legal entities the ownership threshold is lowered to 10 percent. Complex ownership structures with more than three layers must be documented with an ownership chart and reviewed by Financial Crime Compliance before account opening.

## 5. Enhanced Due Diligence

Enhanced Due Diligence (EDD) is mandatory for all High risk customers. EDD must include: verification of source of funds and source of wealth; adverse media screening; understanding of the purpose and intended nature of the relationship; and senior management approval before the relationship is established. For politically exposed persons (PEPs), approval must be obtained from a Managing Director or above in the business line and from the Head of Financial Crime Compliance. Family members and known close associates of PEPs are subject to the same EDD requirements.

## 6. Simplified Due Diligence

Simplified Due Diligence may be applied only to Low risk customers that fall within approved categories, such as listed companies on a regulated exchange and government bodies. Simplified Due Diligence never applies when there is a suspicion of money laundering or terrorist financing, regardless of the customer category.

## 7. Periodic Review

Customer due diligence information must be refreshed on a risk-based cycle: High risk customers every 12 months, Medium risk customers every 24 months, and Low risk customers every 36 months. A periodic review is overdue if it is not completed within 60 days after its due date. Overdue High risk reviews must be escalated to the Head of Financial Crime Compliance, and the business must consider restricting the account until the review is complete.

## 8. Event-Driven Review

An event-driven review must be triggered within 10 business days when any of the following occurs: a material change in ownership or control; a new adverse media hit; a sanctions or PEP screening alert confirmed as a true match; a Suspicious Activity Report filing; or transaction activity materially inconsistent with the customer profile.

## 9. Exit and Restriction

Where the bank cannot complete customer due diligence, it must not open the account, or, for an existing customer, must restrict and consider exiting the relationship. Decisions to exit a High risk customer must be approved by the Customer Exit Committee and documented in the case management system.

## 10. Record Keeping

All customer identification records, verification evidence, risk assessments and EDD documentation must be retained for at least five years after the business relationship ends, in line with the Records Retention and Data Privacy Standard (REC-007).
