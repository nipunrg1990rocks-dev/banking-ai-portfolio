# Operational Risk and Incident Management Policy

This is a synthetic policy for the fictional Ledgerline Bank. It is used for demonstration only.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy defines how Ledgerline Bank identifies, assesses, records and responds to operational risk events and incidents, including process failures, system outages, data errors, fraud and third-party failures.

## 2. Incident Severity Classification

Incidents are classified into four severities. Severity 1 (Critical): significant customer harm, regulatory breach, or estimated loss above 1 million US dollars. Severity 2 (High): material service disruption, potential regulatory impact, or estimated loss between 250,000 and 1 million US dollars. Severity 3 (Medium): limited disruption with loss between 25,000 and 250,000 US dollars. Severity 4 (Low): minor issues with loss below 25,000 US dollars.

## 3. Incident Reporting Timelines

Severity 1 incidents must be reported to the Head of Operational Risk and the Chief Risk Officer within 2 hours of identification, and logged in the incident management system within 24 hours. Severity 2 incidents must be logged within 24 hours. Severity 3 and 4 incidents must be logged within 5 business days. Late logging of an incident is itself an issue that must be recorded.

## 4. Root Cause Analysis

A root cause analysis must be completed for every Severity 1 and Severity 2 incident within 30 calendar days of incident closure. The analysis must identify the failed or missing control and an owner for each corrective action.

## 5. Risk and Control Self-Assessment

Every business unit must complete a Risk and Control Self-Assessment (RCSA) at least annually and after any major change such as a new product, system migration or reorganisation. Controls rated ineffective must have an action plan with a target date no later than 90 days.

## 6. Key Risk Indicators

Business units must maintain Key Risk Indicators (KRIs) with green, amber and red thresholds. A KRI that is red for two consecutive months must be escalated to the Operational Risk Committee.

## 7. Issue Management

Issues identified by audit, regulators, risk functions or self-identification must be logged with an owner, a risk rating and a remediation date. High rated issues past their remediation date must be reported to the Executive Risk Committee. Extensions to remediation dates require approval from the Head of Operational Risk.

## 8. Loss Data Collection

All operational losses of 10,000 US dollars or more must be recorded in the loss event database, including near misses where the potential loss would have exceeded 100,000 US dollars.
