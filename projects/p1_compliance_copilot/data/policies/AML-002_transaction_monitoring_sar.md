# Anti-Money Laundering Transaction Monitoring and Suspicious Activity Reporting Policy

This is a synthetic policy written for the fictional Ledgerline Bank. It is used for demonstration only.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This policy defines how Ledgerline Bank detects, investigates and reports potentially suspicious activity. It applies to all accounts, payment channels and products, including wire transfers, ACH, cards, cash and digital wallets.

## 2. Monitoring Scenarios

The transaction monitoring system runs automated scenarios including: structuring (multiple cash deposits just below the reporting threshold), rapid movement of funds, high-risk jurisdiction transfers, dormant account reactivation, and unusual activity compared with the customer's expected profile. Scenario thresholds must be tuned at least annually, with below-the-line testing documented and approved by the Model Risk Management function.

## 3. Alert Triage and Investigation Timelines

Level 1 analysts must triage every alert within 5 business days of generation. Alerts that cannot be closed at triage are escalated to a Level 2 investigation case. A Level 2 investigation must be completed within 30 calendar days of alert generation. Alert closure must include a written rationale; closures without a rationale are treated as quality assurance failures. A 10 percent random sample of closed alerts is reviewed monthly by AML Quality Assurance.

## 4. Suspicious Activity Report Filing

When an investigator determines that activity is suspicious, a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) must be filed no later than 30 calendar days after the date of initial determination. If no suspect can be identified, filing may be delayed by an additional 30 days to identify a suspect, but in no case may filing exceed 60 calendar days after initial detection. Continuing activity reviews must be performed every 90 days after a SAR is filed, and a continuing SAR filed if the activity persists. The SAR decision must be approved by the AML Officer or a delegated SAR Committee member.

## 5. Prohibition on Tipping Off

Employees must never disclose to a customer, or to any person involved in the transaction, that a SAR has been or may be filed, or that an investigation is under way. Breaches of SAR confidentiality are a serious disciplinary matter and must be reported immediately to the AML Officer. Customer-facing staff who receive questions about account restrictions must use the approved holding script and refer the case to Financial Crime Compliance.

## 6. Currency Transaction Reporting

Cash transactions exceeding 10,000 US dollars in a single business day, individually or in aggregate for the same customer, must be reported through a Currency Transaction Report (CTR) within 15 calendar days. Deliberately splitting cash transactions to avoid the reporting threshold is structuring and must be investigated as potentially suspicious activity.

## 7. Escalation of Urgent Matters

Activity that suggests terrorist financing, human trafficking or an ongoing fraud against a vulnerable customer must be escalated to the AML Officer on the same business day, in addition to any SAR filing obligation. The AML Officer decides whether law enforcement must be contacted immediately.

## 8. Management Information

The AML Officer reports monthly to the Financial Crime Risk Committee on alert volumes, aging of alerts and cases, SAR volumes, timeliness of filings and quality assurance results. Any SAR filed late is reported as a policy breach to the Committee.

## 9. Record Retention

SARs and all supporting documentation must be retained for five years from the date of filing. SAR records must be stored in the restricted SAR repository with access limited to authorised Financial Crime Compliance staff.
